# PROLOGUE

In India, John Herncastle took a famous yellow diamond from a palace during the storming of Seringapatam. A dying guardian cursed the stone and promised vengeance on Herncastle and anyone who received it. Herncastle returned to England with the diamond and refused to explain what happened in the armoury.

# CHAPTER I. The Record

Gabriel Betteredge is asked to record the strange history of the Moonstone. The story is to be told by several witnesses, each limiting the account to what they personally saw. Betteredge remembers that the diamond came to Lady Verinder's Yorkshire house two years earlier and disappeared within a single night.

# CHAPTER II. The Birthday

Rachel Verinder receives the diamond as a birthday gift. Franklin Blake arrives from abroad, and Rachel's cousin Godfrey Ablewhite is also present. Betteredge notices three Indian visitors watching the house, but he cannot decide whether they are pilgrims, thieves, or merely strangers drawn by rumor.

# CHAPTER III. The Missing Stone

During the birthday gathering, Franklin sees Rachel place the Moonstone in her room. Later, he tells Betteredge that he never entered the room again. In the morning, the diamond is gone. Rachel insists that Franklin was with her after midnight, although Betteredge remembers seeing Franklin alone near the staircase.

# CHAPTER IV. The First Inquiry

Sergeant Cuff examines the house. He suspects that the paint on Rachel's door and a smear on Franklin's sleeve may connect him to the theft. Franklin denies touching the door. Rachel refuses to answer questions about the night, while Godfrey urges everyone to protect her reputation.

# CHAPTER V. The New Account

Years later, Franklin claims that he has been searching for the truth. He says he never knew where the diamond went, but he remembers waking outside Rachel's room with mud on his boots. Betteredge privately believes Franklin is hiding something, though he cannot say whether Franklin is guilty, confused, or protecting Rachel.

# EPILOGUE

The family papers remain incomplete. The diamond may have been stolen, hidden, or carried away by someone who believed they were preventing a greater crime. The surviving witnesses agree on the broad outline but disagree about what Franklin knew and when he knew it.
