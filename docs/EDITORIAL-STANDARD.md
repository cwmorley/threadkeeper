# Editorial output standard

## Required writer-facing result

An assessment should answer: What should I change, why, and what else does that change affect?

Each material recommendation includes:

1. A concrete finding or author decision.
2. Original manuscript evidence with chapter and source reference.
3. Classification: observation, attributed claim, interpretation, proposed revision, or unresolved question.
4. Confidence and scope of coverage.
5. A specific edit or a decision needed before an edit is possible.
6. The downstream scenes, clues, knowledge states, or timing assumptions affected.
7. A check that would confirm the revised manuscript works.

The report also identifies passages or mechanisms worth retaining. Rewrites are examples or proposals until applied by author instruction.

## Example acceptance case: Roger Ackroyd

The proposed change is to make Sheppard innocent while preserving events, clues, alibis, and the final reveal.

An adequate analysis must:

- Identify that the requested innocence conflicts with the explicit final confession.
- Retrieve the Chapter IV departure/call, Chapter V discovery, and Chapters XXV–XXVII explanation and confession. Other chapters may be needed; these are minimum anchors, not exhaustive coverage.
- Trace the call, hidden dictaphone, chair, bag, missing time, mechanical skill, and motive as connected dependencies.
- Explain the implications of Sheppard's concealment of Ralph in XXIV.
- Recognize Flora's corrected testimony in XIX–XX as intentional deception with an acknowledged effect on the timeline.
- Give a workable alternative revision brief and at least one example edit with its resulting obligations.
- Avoid inventing a fully solved replacement mystery when motive, opportunity, and evidence have not been established.

Reference deliverable: [editorial assessment](../examples/roger-ackroyd-editorial-assessment.md). This is a qualitative acceptance case; automated semantic evaluation is not yet implemented.

## Coverage and provenance

Record the source, source version/hash when available, selected chapters, truncations, and analysis origin. A whole-book claim requires whole-book evidence coverage. Diagnostics alone cannot establish tone consistency, plot causality, or contradiction.

A revision report must distinguish direct contradictions, necessary downstream changes, optional improvements, and uncertain effects. Similar names and keyword overlap are retrieval hints, not proof of a causal relationship.
